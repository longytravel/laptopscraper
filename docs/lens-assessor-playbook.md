# Lens Assessor Playbook

Use this as the specialist knowledge layer before any model fine-tuning.

## Pricing Rule

AI does not invent price. Value comes from sold comps for the exact lens identity:

- brand
- focal length
- aperture
- mount
- version
- working condition

AI can adjust confidence and flag whether comps are valid.

## Version Matching

Treat these as different products unless sold comps prove otherwise:

- Sigma EX DG Macro vs Sigma EX DG HSM vs Sigma Art.
- Canon EF L USM versions vs non-L or f/4 versions.
- Sony FE full-frame vs Sony E APS-C.
- Nikon F AF-S vs Nikon Z.
- Tamron older DSLR versions vs newer mirrorless Di III versions.

## Condition Penalties

Severe:

- fungus
- haze
- mold
- scratches on glass
- autofocus fault
- aperture fault
- stabilization fault
- dropped
- untested
- spares or repair

Medium:

- stiff zoom or focus ring
- AF not smooth or not accurate
- `*READ*` caveat in title
- no test body
- poor photos
- stock photos only
- missing rear cap

Cosmetic:

- paint coming off
- paint loss
- heavy cosmetic wear
- marks on body
- wear to barrel

Cosmetic wear reduces resale value but does not make a lens bad by itself if optics, autofocus, aperture, and zoom/focus rings are confirmed good.

## Positive Signals

- exact model and mount shown
- clear front and rear glass photos
- serial/model text visible
- front and rear caps
- hood/box/case included
- returns accepted
- seller with strong feedback
- explicit no fungus/haze/scratches statement

## Buyer Questions

Ask before buying:

- Is there fungus, haze, scratches, separation, or oil on aperture blades?
- Does autofocus work accurately?
- Are zoom and focus rings smooth?
- Is the aperture stopping down correctly?
- Are front and rear caps included?
- Can the seller provide front glass, rear glass, mount, and model-text photos?
